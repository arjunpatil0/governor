## Correction to journal 0023: SA2's k = 0.395 was a regressor artefact
Journal 0023 regressed slice stretch on each cell's co-run fg_gpu_p50_ms (inflated under time-slicing, journals 0016/0019: 2.20 ms for the 1 ms fg and 9.68 ms for the 4 ms fg, against 0.99 and 3.99 alone). The registered text says "the fg kernel's gpu service time across fg sizes", i.e. the intended/alone service time. With each size's same-block fg-ALONE fg_gpu_p50 as the regressor (0.1 ms: 0.087; 1 ms: 0.989; 4 ms: 3.986 ms): **k = 0.983 (90% CI [0.974, 0.992]), s0 = 0.243 ms [0.240, 0.245]** (100 cell-blocks). Both versions are in data/w2_A_secondary_results.md. Consequence: the "SA2's k is far from 1" half of the pre-stated globaltimer trigger was met by the artefact. I wrote in journal 0023 that the trigger was met and I counted that half; that was wrong. The SA4 half is separate and stands (below). The finding itself: the background stretches by about the foreground's alone service time plus about 0.24 ms, consistent with plain time-sharing plus a constant switch cost; no mechanism claimed.

## Re-binned capped-wait curve against the unstretched slice end (exploratory, not independent)
Unstretched end = slice start + the cell's median duration of slices with no fg completion; requests landing after it (in the stretch tail) are dropped (56,704 of 59,985 points kept). Results: the sparse bins (0.35-0.55 ms) and the 0.154 ms dip at 0.60-0.65 ms are gone (every bin >= 700 requests), so the original dip was at least partly an x-axis artefact (the reviewer's explanation). But the curve still ripples: mean wait 0.186 ms at 0-0.05 ms, rising to 0.300 at 0.30-0.35, falling to 0.214 at 0.35-0.40, rising to 0.364 at 0.65-0.70, falling to 0.232 at 0.80-0.85, then about 0.28-0.33 ms beyond 1 ms; period about 0.45 ms. The capped-wait fit on this axis: tau = 0.190 ms [0.180, 0.200], offset 0.111 ms, with tau depending on m (0.20 / 0.18 / 0.13 at m = 2 / 4 / 8). On the observed axis (first report) tau was 0.260 [0.260, 0.270]. The capped-wait model is not a single clean shape: its tau depends on the x-axis definition and on m. The ripple is unexplained; a ripple of period 0.45 ms next to a quantum of order 0.5-0.9 ms is a possible link, post hoc, to be tested by the on-GPU timeline, not asserted.

## A's thermal sensitivity, and a bug
39 of 190 A cells are start_temp_not_reached (blocks 1, 2, 7, 8, 9; 36 are non-baseline). Two sensitivity runs were added to the A report: (1) drop the 36 flagged non-baseline cells (baselines kept, because every block needs its baseline); (2) also drop every block whose baseline was flagged (3 blocks; 61 cells dropped in total).
- HA1: unchanged in both (slope 0.013, CI [0.012, 0.013] / [0.013, 0.014], 17% of cells within 25%, classification preempted_or_concurrent).
- HA2: NOT SUPPORTED in both, 1/6 significant (m = 0.25 only). The m = 0.5 and 1.0 effects keep their sign and size (median diffs +0.017 and +0.011 ms) but are left with 6 complete block pairs, where the smallest possible one-sided Wilcoxon p is 1/64 = 0.0156, which after Holm (x6) is 0.094 > 0.05: they cannot be significant. The change from 3/6 to 1/6 is lost power, not a reversal.
- **Bug found on the way (my error):** my first thermal sensitivity run printed "HA2 SUPPORTED (6/6)" with p = NaN in the table. Cause: ha2 paired blocks with a pivot table; a block missing one cv cell gave a NaN difference, the Wilcoxon returned NaN, and Holm turned NaN into 0, so those m values counted as significant. Fix test-first (tests/test_w2_analysis.py::test_ha2_ignores_incomplete_blocks_instead_of_producing_nan_p_values: RED, then GREEN): ha2 uses complete block pairs only, n shown per m. The registered all-cell result has no missing cells and is unchanged (verified by diff of the report body). Full suite: 113 passed before the fix, 114 after.
