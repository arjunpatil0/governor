## B review (reviews/2026-10-21_w2_B_review.md; response in reviews/2026-10-21_w2_B_response.md)
- **Finding 1 (design lesson).** HB2 with the memory background is uninformative by construction: a saturating background (duty 1) plus a foreground that needs about 53% of the GPU is above 100% demand at any clock, and the 1800 MHz reference already misses ~100% of frames (ceiling). The registered verdict (NOT SUPPORTED) is kept; the report now labels it "UNINFORMATIVE: ceiling". Lesson: a clock-effect arm must have a reference that meets its deadlines; the co-location arms need a paced background (duty < 1), which is what A2's duty sweep measures.
- **Finding 2 (kernel-specific knees).** The memory-frame HB1 miss at 900 MHz (observed 1.011 vs the W1-derived 1.123) is not a memory-clock effect: W1 logged 8001 MHz at 900/1200, and B locks 8001 MHz. W1's streaming kernel was clock-limited below about 1200 MHz; B's memory-frame kernel is not (compute per byte differs). So a W1 ratio does not transfer between two kernels that are both called "memory-bound". Same lesson as Experiment C (the int4 and fp16 proxies, and a real engine, will each have their own knee): **the knee is a property of the specific kernel, not of a workload class**, so a governor must measure it per workload (or learn it); it cannot be looked up from a class label. This is stated as an interpretation of one kernel pair; the W1/B kernel difference was not isolated by a further experiment.
- **Finding 3 (what fg_gpu_p50_ms measures).** The foreground records a cudaEvent before the kernel and one after it; the interval therefore runs from when the GPU reaches the first event to when it reaches the second, and while the context is time-sliced it can include switched-out time. In co-run cells the report labels the column "interval incl. possible switched-out time" and says it must not be read as service time. The observed pattern (12.13 ms at 1800 with the background vs 12.10 without, but 22.2 vs 8.86 ms unlocked) is NOT explained yet; an Nsight Systems trace of fma_60hz_membg at 1800 and unlocked is requested (experiments/w2_colocation/trace_cell.py, admin). Its result goes into a new entry. No co-run service time is interpreted until then.
- **Finding 4.** Per-cell table now has frac_completed next to the latency percentiles, with a sentence that percentiles cover completed frames only.
- **Finding 5.** The first launch (20261021T104818Z) was interrupted by accident by Arjun (journal 0016); 20261021T105205Z is the analysed run.
- **Finding 6 (descriptive, not a tested claim): deadline-aware clock locking.** With slack, a lower lock saves board power at zero misses. fma_60hz no background: 56.5 W unlocked, 38.5 W at 1800, 31.9 W at 1500 MHz (p99 15.0 ms against the 16.7 ms budget, 0% misses; 43% less power, but only about 1.7 ms of p99 margin); 1200 MHz and below miss. mem_60hz no background: 24.9 W at 900 MHz vs 40.7 W unlocked, latency unchanged (9.10 vs 8.99 ms p50). The report now generates this table (lowest-power level with zero median misses per frame). Design implication for the governor: pick the lowest clock whose predicted service time fits the deadline with margin, per workload (see finding 2). Whether this is "slack reclamation DVFS" in the real-time literature (reviewer cites Pillai and Shin, SOSP 2001) is unverified here; not cited until checked.
