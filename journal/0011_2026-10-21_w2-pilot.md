# 0011 — W2 pilot: what ran, what the numbers say, what it changes in the design
Date: 2026-10-21 · Phase: W2 (pilot, before pre-registration) · Author: Claude Code
## Question
Is the W2 design runnable (two processes, shared QPC timeline, slice generator, foreground release, decode proxy), and what do the
calibration numbers say about frame sizing and the baseline? The pilot sets kernel unit counts only; it must not set hypotheses or thresholds (0008).

  Both unlocked, no admin, tree dirty with the new files (pilot only). Stall diagnostic: `data/pilot/w2/diagnostics/` (script + output).
## What happened (all numbers from the files above)
- **P0:** HAGS NVIDIA adapter `DriverSupportState:Stable Enabled:True` (parsed from `dxdiag /t`). `perf_counter()` vs the DLL's QPC: max skew 4.5 µs over 1000 reads, so the two processes can share a timeline.
- **P1/P2 slice generator:** realised mean within ±5% of nominal for 11 of 12 cells (mem 0.25 ms deterministic: +14.8%, an intercept effect of the calibration); realised CV 0.97–1.02 for CV 1 and ≤0.03 for CV 0.
  ρ = 0.998–0.9998 and inter-slice gaps are 0.4–0.7 µs mean (≤ 5.6 µs p99) because the loop is in C++. So the back-to-back background assumption (ρ ≈ 1) holds in the harness. The analysis uses realised moments, not nominal.
- **P3 isolated foreground is clock-state dependent.** A "1 ms" kernel (calibrated at boost) ran at p50 1.4–1.6 ms in isolation (SM clock 1305–1980 MHz, median ~1500, at ~20% duty), and a "0.1 ms" kernel at p50 0.86 ms
  when run cold. The GPU idles at 210 MHz / 5 W and does not boost for a light load.
- **P3 stalls are a cold-start effect, not noise.** In `stall_diag2_output.txt` the 1 ms runs that begin right after an idle gap show a 57–95-request backlog (worst latency 172 / 314 ms; first kernels took ~25 ms) beginning at request 0 or 1; every run that starts warm has none (max ≤ 9 ms).
  The first request after idle runs at 210 MHz until clocks ramp. Any measurement window needs a warm-up excluded from the statistics.
- **P4 frame sizing:** fma frame unlocked service time 8.86 ms at 60 Hz (53% of the 16.67 ms budget; 185,633 units) and 4.42 ms at 120 Hz (53% of 8.33 ms; 92,586 units); mem frames 8.92 / 4.48 ms (53–54%). Inside the 50–60% target (0008 mod B). (The fma 60 Hz run had p99 124–165 ms in both pilot runs, probably the same cold-start effect; not separately diagnosed.)
- **P5 (one co-run cell, feasibility only):** fma bg, 2 ms constant slices (realised mean 2.13 ms, ρ 0.9997); fg 0.1 ms kernel, 200 Hz Poisson, 2000 requests, two processes, fg on CPU 4 at high priority, bg on CPU 8.
  - Residual-wait prediction ρ·E[S²]/(2E[S]) = 1.073 ms.
  - Observed fg latency: p50 0.40 ms, mean 0.43 ms, p99 1.24 ms, max 2.17 ms; fg kernel GPU time p50 0.086 ms (full speed).
  - Latency barely depends on where in the bg slice the arrival lands: mean 0.47 / 0.46 / 0.42 / 0.39 ms for arrivals in the 0–0.5 / 0.5–1 / 1–1.5 / 1.5+ ms of the slice (run 2; run 1: 0.47 / 0.46 / 0.46 / 0.38), versus a non-preemptive wait that would shrink from ~2 ms to ~0 across those bins.
  - bg slices that contain an fg completion are longer: mean 2.34 ms (n 1548) versus 2.03 ms (n 7261) in run 2 (run 1: 2.33 ms, n 1578, versus 2.03 ms, n 7177).
  - Both from `data/pilot/w2/diagnostics/p5_phase_analysis.py`, outputs saved beside it; the two runs replicate.
  - Reading: this looks like the GPU preempting or interleaving the bg slice for the fg kernel, not the fg waiting out the slice (a "preempted / concurrent" signature in the plan's classification). One cell, one run, unregistered: not evidence for or against HA1.
  - The pilot's own "wait = latency − isolated p50" is meaningless here (negative) because the isolated baseline ran at ramping clocks (see P3).
- **P6 decode proxy** (unlocked, 8 launches per layer; effective GB/s = bytes read per token × tokens/s):
  | variant | layers | launches/token | graph | ms/token p50 | tok/s | MB/token | eff. GB/s |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | int4 | 28 | 224 | no | 9.98 | 100.5 | 1468 | 147.5 |
  | int4 | 28 | 224 | yes | 9.65 | 103.9 | 1468 | 152.6 |
  | fp16 | 14 | 112 | no | 12.01 | 83.5 | 2848 | 237.8 |
  | fp16 | 14 | 112 | yes | 11.80 | 85.0 | 2848 | 242.0 |
  Residual stream finite in all four. fp16 runs at about the W1 streaming bandwidth (~232 GB/s); int4 reaches only ~64% of that, and a CUDA graph helps by 3.4% (int4) / 1.8% (fp16), so launch cost is not what limits it. That fits the dequantisation work making int4 less memory-bound, but only a clock sweep (Experiment C) can show a knee.
## Interpretation
1. A must run at a **fixed SM clock** (admin): unlocked, the fg service time depends on how busy the GPU just was, so the "isolated" baseline and the co-run cell sit at different clocks. Baseline must be measured under the same lock, after a warm-up.
2. Every cell needs a warm-up/preroll excluded from the statistics (cold-start backlog is up to ~370 ms).
3. If the P5 signature repeats in the registered run, HA1 as pre-registered will be rejected and the interesting result is the bg stretch per fg request (a preemption cost); the plan already lists "preempted/concurrent" as a classified failure of HA1.
## Setbacks / surprises
- My own ctypes bug (missing `c_double`) crashed run 1 at P6; the parts before it were valid and kept.
- The 100–400 ms stalls looked like system noise until a targeted diagnostic placed them at request 0–1 after idle.
## Decisions
None yet. Proposed for Arjun: (a) A at a locked SM clock; (b) cold-start warm-up excluded; (c) whether to add a pre-registered secondary measuring bg stretch per fg request. ADR-0008 follows the registration.
