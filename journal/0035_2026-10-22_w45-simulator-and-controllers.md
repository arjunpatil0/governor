**Results (data/w45_sim_results.md; evaluation seeds 200-229; all simulation).**
- Feasibility gate (tuning seeds): no governor misses 33.8% of frames in S1 (+/-30% steps); a best static setting of either pacing holds the SLO. Passed.
- SLO: a fails S1, S1d, S3. Aligned controllers b', c', d' hold in all six scenarios. c holds (S3 at 90% of runs), d holds; b (unaligned static) fails S3 (infeasible even on tuning seeds).
- Learned vs feedback (pre-registered rule): d beats c and d' beats c' in S1 and S4 with CIs above 0 and no loss in S0/S2/S3, so under the rule the learned part "earns its place". Caveat: c is a weak baseline (S0: 6.9 tok/s at 34.8 W while the static unaligned setting gets 60 tok/s); unaligned feedback with 10 ms tokens oscillates between misses and back-off, and the only parameter sets that held the SLO in every scenario were very conservative (7 of 108 combinations).
- Against the best static setting the dynamic controllers do NOT win on the objective (tok/s - 0.5 W): c' and d' win only in S2 (fg idle phases: knee clock, +3.8 / +4.0) and lose in S1, S1d, S4 (c' S1: 28.3 tok/s at 45.2 W vs b' 53.9 tok/s at 74.4 W). Every per-scenario best static setting chose the unlocked clock; aligned ones chose guard 0 ms. So in this plant, slack-filling pacing at full clock is the strongest simple policy; clock lowering trades 20-25 tok/s for 25-30 W (a Pareto trade, not a dominance), and aligned pacing beats unaligned at equal SLO (b' minus b: wins in S0, S1, S1d, S2, S3).
- Caveats that favour the static aligned setting: the plant has no bg token-time jitter (guard 0 is optimal), a single fg, and an objective weight lambda = 0.5 tok/s per W fixed before the runs. Hardware will have token jitter, thermal behaviour and unmodelled contention.
