## What the traces contain (analysis/nsys_queue_summary.py, output data/nsys/queue_summary.txt; sqlite exported locally from the .nsys-rep)
- **Both processes appear in both traces**: two python.exe CUDA contexts (TARGET_INFO_CUDA_CONTEXT_INFO: pids 16420 and 35064 at 1800; 2108 and 9876 unlocked), each submitting WDDM queue packets to the 3D engine. The lower-volume process (1409 / 1779 packets) is consistent with the foreground and the higher-volume one (17,821 / 20,339) with the background; this assignment is an INFERENCE from packet volume (the trace carries no process-role label).
- Their queue packets are in flight at the same time (1409 of 1409 packets of the lower-volume process overlap an in-flight packet of the other at 1800; 1779 of 1779 unlocked). That shows both contexts have work queued concurrently, NOT that kernels execute concurrently.
- **What the traces do NOT contain**: (a) no CUDA kernel activity table (CUPTI_ACTIVITY_KIND_KERNEL is absent; only 4 runtime-API records): CUPTI flushes its buffers at process exit and my fixed trace_cell.py kills the children after they write their outputs (`killed_after_output`, status files in data/nsys/*/trace_cell_status.json), so the kernel records were lost. Fixing this needs the exit hang of journal 0018 to be solved, which it is not. (b) No per-process execution intervals: the WDDM DMA-packet events are attributed to System/dwm/csrss contexts, none to our contexts, and WDDM_HW_QUEUE_EVENTS has only 24 rows (queue creation, 12 per process); `preempted` is 0 on all 1206 DMA packets. With HAGS ON (journal 0010) the GPU schedules in hardware, so the OS-level trace cannot show our context switches. **The traces therefore cannot show when each context was switched in or out.**
- Tracing inflated the workload (fg_gpu_ms 30.8 vs 12.1 ms untraced, journal 0018), so nothing in these traces is a result.
## What fg_gpu_ms measures (from the code, w2_kernels.cu: w2_fg_run; not from the trace)
fg_gpu_ms is cudaEventElapsedTime(e0, e1): both events are recorded on the foreground's stream, one before and one after the kernel launch, and each event is time-stamped when the GPU reaches it in that stream's order.
- It EXCLUDES any wait before e0 is reached (for example while the other process holds the GPU): that wait appears in end-to-end latency (t_complete - arrival) but not in fg_gpu_ms.
- It INCLUDES any time between e0 and e1 during which the context is switched out. It is "elapsed GPU-timeline interval", equal to service time only if the context is not switched out in between.
- Observed (B, medians): fma_60hz at 1800 MHz 12.13 ms with the background vs 12.10 alone (no inflation), unlocked 22.2 vs 8.86 ms, mem frames 24.2 vs 8.94 ms (2.5-2.7x).
- **Why 1800 differs from unlocked remains UNEXPLAINED.** A single "the kernel is split by a time-slice" story would predict inflation at 1800 too (the kernel is longer there, 12 ms vs 8.9 ms), and the data show the opposite; the traces cannot adjudicate it. I offer no mechanism.
## Consequences
- No co-run fg_gpu_ms is interpreted as service time anywhere. Co-run claims use end-to-end latency, miss fraction and completed fraction only (labelled in the B report).
- Not claimed: that the OS or GPU preempts mid-kernel at any quantum, or what the quantum is.
- If Arjun wants the mechanism: a diagnostic that does not depend on tracing is to record %globaltimer at the first block start and last block end inside the foreground kernel (kernel-side execution span) beside the event interval, fg alone vs fg+bg at 1800 and unlocked. That is a new descriptive diagnostic and needs an addendum written before it runs; it is NOT done. Recommendation: skip unless the governor needs co-run service times; A/A2 are more decision-relevant.
- Trace files are large (38 and 41 MB; the local sqlite exports are 0.73-0.79 GB): *.nsys-rep and *.sqlite under data/nsys are gitignored; the .nsys-rep files stay on disk at data/nsys/; the summary script output and status files are committed.
