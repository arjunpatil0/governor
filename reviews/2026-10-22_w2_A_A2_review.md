## Findings
1. [RE-ANALYSE] SA2's k = 0.395 is probably an artefact of the regressor. analysis/w2_analysis.py:503 uses r.fg_gpu_p50_ms from
   the CO-RUN cell, and B showed that interval is inflated under time-slicing (here 2.20 ms for the 1 ms fg and 9.68 ms for the 4 ms fg,
   vs 0.99 / 3.99 alone). The yaml defines the regressor as the fg kernel's service time across sizes {0.1, 1, 4 ms}, which is the
   intended/alone service time. Back-of-envelope with the fitted line: 0.30 + 0.395*9.68 ≈ 4.12 ms of stretch for the 4 ms fg →
   (4.12−0.30)/3.99 ≈ 0.96; for 1 ms: (0.30+0.395*2.20−0.30)/0.99 ≈ 0.88. So with alone service times, k is likely ≈ 0.9–1.0, which is
   consistent with plain time-slicing (the background yields for the fg's run time). Recompute SA2 with each size's same-block
   alone fg_gpu_p50 as x. Report both: the computation as run, and the corrected one with the reason.
   If corrected k ≈ 1, the "k far from 1" half of the globaltimer trigger was met by an artefact (record that in the journal).
2. [RE-ANALYSE] The dip in the binned capped-wait curve (0.154 ms at 0.60–0.65 ms before slice end, sparse bins at 0.35–0.55)
   looks like an artefact of the x-axis. "Time to slice end" uses the host-observed slice end, and slices that contain an fg
   completion are stretched by ~0.3+ ms (SA2). A request arriving r ms before the *unstretched* end, in a slice that then gets stretched,
   is placed at r + stretch on the axis, while its wait stays ≈ r. That predicts a shifted copy of the 0–0.3 ms ramp, which is what
   bins 0.6–1.0 look like (0.154 → 0.353). Check: recompute time-to-end against an unstretched end (slice start + the cell's median
   duration of slices with no fg completion), and see whether the dip disappears. If it does, the capped-wait shape is cleaner than
   reported. Still exploratory and not independent (same data).
3. [FIX] The thermal sensitivity for A is missing. 39/190 A cells are start_temp_not_reached (blocks 1, 2, 7, 8, 9). The A2 report
   does the incident sensitivity, but A's report doesn't do the thermal one. Add HA1/HA2 with those cells excluded (verdicts unchanged
   by rule; for transparency).
4. [NOTE] What survives as findings (descriptive):
   - Small fg requests: added wait is bounded at ~0.26–0.30 ms regardless of background slice length (A), independent of where in the
     slice they arrive (SA1), not changed by process priority (SA3). Within the last ~0.3 ms of a slice, wait ≈ time remaining (ramp,
     bins 0–0.3 ms).
   - Large fg work is time-shared: slowdown at duty 1 is 2.65 (1 ms) and 2.91 (4 ms), above the 2.0 that loss-free equal sharing
     implies. A simple quantum-q / switch-cost-s model gives slowdown 2 + 2s/q, so s ≈ 0.3 ms implies q ≈ 0.7–0.9 ms. That's a hypothesis
     for the diagnostic, not a finding.
   - Governor-relevant operating point: pacing the background to duty 0.25 keeps the 12 ms (at 1800 MHz) 60 Hz frame inside its
     deadline (p99 15.6 ms < 16.7), while duty ≥ 0.5 overloads it. So duty cycle, not slice size, is the knob that controls
     foreground QoS on this platform.
5. [NOTE] The lower board power after the two stalls is plausibly lower leakage on a cooler die. Only worth a line; no claim.
