One graphics chip, two jobs

A game-like app has to draw a new picture every 16.7 ms. An AI chatbot running on the same laptop wants the chip too. This tour shows, step by step, what happens when they share it, and how a manager program decides who gets the chip.

PictureOne frame the app draws. Must finish before the next one is due.
Word pieceOne token the chatbot writes. About 10 ms each for the 3B model.
Late pictureFinished after its deadline. On screen, that is a stutter.

What the real laptop measured

The same managers, run on the real RTX 4070 laptop GPU in a fair test: 10 rounds in shuffled order, 100 seconds per run, with a 60 W power limit and a target of at most 1 late picture in 100. Bars show chatbot output; the number beside each bar is the share of late pictures.

What it adds up to. Letting both run freely makes about a third of pictures late at around 90 W. The best fixed setting never makes pictures late, but gives the chatbot nothing whenever a word piece doesn't fit between pictures.

The governor gives the chatbot real output in every scene, and wins clearly when the app goes idle (41 vs 32 pieces per second, same lateness). Where pieces don't fit, it has to squeeze them in, which costs some late pictures: 1.7% with load jumps, 4.7% with the large model. By the target set in advance, it did not pass.

How this page works

  • The live chip is a simulation, built from measurements made on the real laptop: how picture time and power change with chip speed, how fast the chatbot writes at each speed, how much both slow down when they share the chip (about 2.5×), and 5 W idle power.
  • The Small, Medium and Phi speeds were measured; the Large (8B) speed is estimated from how fast it ran while sharing. All models use the Medium model's power numbers.
  • The governor here is a simplified copy of the real one: the same search over chip speeds and chatbot rules, the same safety margin and power limit, and the same live corrections. It is told the true picture size instead of estimating it.
  • It matches the real results closely for running freely, for the fixed setting, and for the governor's output with the Medium model. It shows fewer late pictures and lower power than the real governor, and it is too pessimistic for the long word pieces of Phi and the Large model.
Built from the GPU governor project's measurements and its pre-registered evaluation (10 blocks, 425 hardware runs).
